# The Call

*End of sweep. ~450 industries enumerated, 38 survived, 20 deep-dived across two rounds. This file stops surveying and commits.*

---

## 1. The uncomfortable number

**50–70% of business acquisitions fail.** And the cited causes are not bad businesses — they are buyer errors, every one of them controllable:

- inadequate due diligence, specifically failure to independently verify the seller's financial claims
- underestimating **customer concentration**
- not assessing **owner dependency**
- key employees or customers leaving post-close
- integration cost exceeding expectations
- overpaying relative to value received

Read that list against everything in this sweep. **Not one of those failure modes is a vertical-selection problem.** They are all execution problems, and they all happen after the vertical is chosen.

Which means the marginal value of researching vertical #41 is approximately zero, and has been for a while. The analysis is done. What follows is a decision.

## 2. The call: commercial electrical service contracting (NAICS 238210)

### The entry/exit spread is a *mix shift*, not just a scale trade

| Profile | Multiple |
|---|---|
| Owner-operator shop | **3–5x SDE** |
| Mid-market, $1M+ EBITDA | **5–8x EBITDA** |
| $1–5M EBITDA add-on | 5.5–8.0x |
| **Data-centre / mission-critical exposure** | **8.0–12.0x** |
| Data-centre/utility/industrial end-market mix | **+1–2x EBITDA over standard commercial mix** |
| Service-and-maintenance-heavy vs project-heavy | **+0.5–1.0x** |
| Commercial maintenance contracts | **+0.5–0.8 turns** |

Most roll-up arbitrage is "buy small at 4x, be big, sell at 10x" — which requires you to actually get big. **This one doesn't.** You can buy a generalist commercial electrician at 4–5x and re-rate the *same* business by changing what it sells: shift end-market mix toward data-centre/mission-critical, convert project revenue to contracted maintenance. Those two moves are worth roughly **2–3 turns on their own**, before any scale.

That is a far shorter path to value than assembling ten companies.

### The tailwind is not yours to create

Data-centre construction backlog is described as the single largest 2024–2026 tailwind for electrical contractors, and McKinsey projects **global data-centre power demand tripling by 2030.** You are not betting on your own cleverness. You are standing in front of the largest capex wave in the economy holding the one trade it cannot proceed without.

### The timing window is explicit

> **"PE consolidation is active but earlier than HVAC."**

HVAC already ran. Apex (Apollo, $10B), Wrench (Leonard Green), Sila (Goldman, $1.7B), Champions (Blackstone, $2.5B at 18.5x). Bolt-ons there now clear 5–7x and every seller has a banker.

Electrical is the same trade, one cycle behind. Commercial-heavy platform multiples are at decade highs while owner-operator shops still change hands at 3–5x SDE. That gap closes.

### It passes the Part VII test cleanly

**"If AI were free and perfect tomorrow, what is still scarce here?"**

Licensed master electricians. Trade wages are running **5–7% annually**, 530,000 skilled-trade jobs sit unfilled, ~1.4M projected by 2030, and no model shortens a four-year apprenticeship. Licensed-master-electrician depth is named explicitly as a driver of what buyers pay.

Meanwhile the intelligence burden is large and entirely separable: estimating and takeoff, service dispatch and routing, scheduling, submittals, compliance documentation, change-order management, AR. That is the cost you delete. **The AI raises the return on the crews. The crews are the moat.**

### What kills it

- **Customer/GC concentration** — the #1 cited acquisition failure cause, and endemic in contracting
- **Bonding capacity** doesn't automatically transfer; verify before LOI
- **The master electrician's licence** may sit with the departing owner. If the licence walks, the business stops. Structure for it or walk away
- **Project-heavy books are traps** — WIP accounting hides losses, and percentage-of-completion can be gamed. Buy the service base, not the backlog
- **Cyclicality** — commercial construction turns. The service/maintenance base is the hedge; that's why it's worth 0.5–0.8 turns more

## 3. Runner-up: hood cleaning & grease abatement micro-roll-up

Cheapest entry and the best small-cap economics found anywhere in the sweep — **21% EBITDA margins, 88% recurring, NFPA 96-mandated**. Fire code sets the frequency; the fire marshal enforces collection.

It is the purest expression of the Part V financing arbitrage: sub-$500k EBITDA targets trade at 2–4x SDE **because an SBA buyer's debt service can't support more**, not because the businesses are worse. Inside a platform with shared dispatch and back office, the same cash flow supports 5–6x.

**Why it's second, not first:** it is a 30-acquisition programme of $200k–$800k EBITDA targets. That's thirty diligences, thirty integrations, thirty owner relationships — against a failure-rate base of 50–70% per transaction. It's a superb second act once you have an operating spine. It's a punishing first one.

## 5. What actually happens next

The sweep is closed. The failure modes are all downstream of here.

1. **Pick one metro.** Density is the only synergy that is real on deal one.
2. **Build the target list from public data** — state contractor licence registries give you every licensed electrical contractor with licence class, licence age, and qualifying individual. Cross-reference for owners near retirement age. That is the actual first artefact.
3. **Call fifty owners.** Not a banker — the owner. Half the value of this whole document is knowing what to ask, and the other half only appears in those calls.
4. **Diligence in this order, because this is the published failure list:** customer concentration → owner dependency → who holds the licence → bonding → WIP quality → then financials.
5. **Buy the service base, not the backlog.**

The research phase is over. It was over about fifteen verticals ago.
