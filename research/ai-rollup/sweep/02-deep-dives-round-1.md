## 1. Repossession & recovery (NAICS 561491) — 🟢🟢🟢 the standout finding of the sweep

**This is the largest supply/demand imbalance found anywhere in this research programme.**

| | |
|---|---|
| **Demand** | ~**10 million** vehicles assigned for repossession in 2026 |
| **Actual recoveries** | ~**2.6 million** |
| **Gap** | **~7.4 million assignments not recovered** |
| Underlying credit | Subprime auto 60+ delinquency **6.9%, a 32-year record**; overall auto delinquency 3.88%, a 15-year high and above the GFC peak; 3M+ repossessions in 2025, most since the GFC aftermath |
| Structure | Highly fragmented — some agents take thousands of assignments/month, others a couple of hundred. CCRA certification, wrongful-repossession insurance, specialised equipment |
| Industry description | "Feast mode," with forecasts of 10.5M assignments by year-end |

**Why this matters more than the demand story.** Everyone can see delinquency is at a record. What almost nobody has priced is that **the binding constraint is recovery capacity, not assignment volume.** Demand exceeds physical capacity by roughly 4x. That is the purest possible instance of the Part VII thesis: the scarce complement is trucks, licensed agents, secured lot space and drivers — none of which AI creates — while the entire administrative layer (assignment intake, skip tracing, address verification, compliance documentation, lender reporting, invoicing) is exactly what AI absorbs.

**The AI lever here is unusually direct:** in a market where 74% of assignments go unrecovered, throughput per truck *is* the P&L. Better skip tracing and address prioritisation converts directly into recoveries per route-day, on a fleet you already own.

**Pairs structurally with motor vehicle towing (488410)** — same trucks, same drivers, same dispatch, same lots. Towing adds police-rotation and motor-club contracts, which are a permission (municipalities award rotation lists), and its demand is uncorrelated with credit. Repo is the countercyclical upside; towing is the base load.

**Risks:** repossession is politically exposed — predatory-towing legislation is already an active industry topic, and wrongful-repossession liability is severe. Lender-side "forwarders" sit between agents and lenders and compress agent margins. And the demand spike is, by construction, cyclical: underwrite the trough, not 2026.

## 2. Elevator service (within NAICS 238290) — 🟢🟢 biggest multiple spread found

| | |
|---|---|
| Independent contractor entry | **6–11x EBITDA, median ~7.5x** (~1.3x revenue) |
| Scaled independents | **~24–25x EBITDA** |
| Strategic comps | KONE/TK Elevator at **~18.4x** (€29.4B EV, April 2026); APi/Elevated at ~12.9x |
| Competition | **13 active independent consolidators** with named sponsors; Otis alone did ~30 bolt-ons globally in 2024 on $87M invested |

**The value driver is explicit and measurable:** the maintenance-contract portfolio. Every step from project revenue toward contracted, **code-mandated** maintenance revenue has been worth roughly **one to two turns of EBITDA at every size tier.** Revenue is legally required (ASME A17.1 inspection and testing), technicians are union-scarce and licensed, and the installed base is the asset — you service equipment you know and nobody else can price.

**Verdict:** a 7.5x → 24x spread is the widest in this entire research programme. But 13 consolidators plus the OEMs means you are bidding, not discovering. Only worth it off-market, and only if you can staff mechanics.

## 3. Septic, grease trap & portable sanitation (NAICS 562991/562998) — 🟢🟢

| | |
|---|---|
| Entry | **6–8x EBITDA** to PE-backed platforms |
| Active consolidators | SVI (Aurora Capital), Wind River Environmental (Macquarie), Patriot Environmental |
| **Operating metric that decides everything** | **8–10 residential pumps per route-day = strong margin; 4–6 = sub-scale** |
| Value drivers | Service-mix breadth, route density, real-estate-transaction inspection volume, **state licence and NAWT credential count, disposal-access security**, environmental-liability tail |

**Why it qualifies:** demand is non-discretionary and schedule-mandated, ownership is mom-and-pop, and **disposal access is a genuine permission** — you cannot run routes without somewhere legally permitted to dump. Route density is a physical, un-copyable asset.

**The AI lever is real but bounded:** routing/dispatch optimisation moves pumps-per-route-day, which is the single metric the multiple is set on. Moving a book from 6 to 8 pumps/day is a ~30% throughput gain on the same trucks. That's a genuine margin story, not a marketing one.

**Risk:** environmental liability tail is the diligence item — permit-violation history transfers with the entity.

## 4. Commercial & industrial machinery repair (NAICS 811310) — 🟢🟢 the most under-examined block

| | |
|---|---|
| Market | Global commercial & industrial machinery repair/maintenance ~**$712B in 2026**; North American heavy machinery maintenance **$47.8B (2025) → $65.4B (2033)** |
| Fragmentation | ~55–57% held by established providers, **43–45% distributed across regional specialists** |
| Industrial-services buy-box | **$3–30M EBITDA · 5–8x platform · 4–6x add-on · exit 10–12x** |
| **Timing signal** | **Median enterprise multiple compressed from 11.73x (Q1 2025) to 9.96x (Q1 2026)** — entry prices are falling while business-services deal volume rebounded 21% off the Q1 2024 trough |

**Why it qualifies:** recurring industrial service relationships, technician-constrained, and the customer's cost of downtime dwarfs the service fee — so it doesn't shop on price. The intelligence burden (scheduling, parts sourcing, service reporting, PM contract management, quoting) is the entire back office.

**Verdict:** genuinely under-examined relative to its size. The 9.96x median is a *falling* entry price in a market everyone else is bidding up — worth a second pass.

## 5. Biomedical & lab equipment service — independent service organisations (NAICS 811210) — 🟢🟢

| | |
|---|---|
| Market | Medical equipment maintenance **~$52–61B in 2026**, growing **~10% CAGR** to $114–119B by 2033/34 |
| Structure | **OEMs hold 50.6%**; ISOs are the fragmented multi-vendor challengers competing on cost |
| Consolidation | Early — Siemens Healthineers/CommonSpirit acquired Block Imaging (2023) for multi-vendor imaging parts and service |

**Why it qualifies:** hospitals are structurally motivated to move spend off OEM contracts; ISOs win on price and multi-vendor coverage. Revenue is contracted, equipment-specific and sticky. BMETs (biomedical equipment technicians) are scarce and credentialed. **"Fragmented service providers increase administrative burden"** — that phrase, from the market research itself, is the AI thesis stated by the incumbents.

**Risk:** OEMs restrict access to parts, service keys and documentation — a real and active fight. Diligence parts access before anything else.

## 6. Metal finishing, electroplating & anodizing (NAICS 332812/332813) — 🟢🟢 permits as the asset

| | |
|---|---|
| Scale | **~44,000 US facilities** discharging process wastewater under NPDES permits or POTW control mechanisms |
| Regulatory gates | EPA Metal Finishing Effluent Guidelines, NESHAP area-source standards, VOC air permits |
| Consolidation | Valence Surface Technologies (largest independent aerospace surface-finishing platform) acquiring C.I.L. Metal Finishing/Electroplating, Aug 2025; Pioneer Metal Finishing acquired Electrochem Solutions |

**Why it qualifies — textbook Part VII.** The scarce asset is a **wastewater discharge permit plus an operating plating line**, which is effectively impossible to obtain de novo in most jurisdictions. New entrants don't happen. Aerospace/defence work adds NADCAP and customer-specific approvals that take years. Meanwhile the intelligence burden — quoting, job routing, certifications, compliance reporting, environmental documentation — is heavy and entirely separable.

**Risk:** environmental liability is the whole diligence. You inherit the site history. This needs Phase II work before an LOI, not after.

## 7. Claims adjusting, TPA & insurer-paid services (NAICS 524291/524298) — 🟢

| | |
|---|---|
| Entry | Platform TPA ~**9x EBITDA**; platforms and add-ons averaged **7.2x** in H1 2025 |
| Consolidation | Active — IAS Claim Services, Johns Eastern, Brown & Brown's TPA/claims businesses acquired; Cor Partners buy-and-build across loss adjusting, forensic engineering and consulting; Intercare acquired by Aquiline (2025) |
| Signal | "Many firms that are mid-consolidators will seek exits" — secondary supply coming |

**Verdict:** confirms Part V's insurer-paid thesis but **downgrades it on price and crowding.** At 7–9x with named PE platforms already mid-cycle, this is not the quiet corner it looked like. The independent-adjusting and premium-audit/loss-control sub-segments remain more fragmented than the workers'-comp TPA core — that's where to look if at all.

## 8. Construction materials testing & special inspection (within NAICS 541380/541350) — 🟢 confirmed structure, thin data

**The regulatory structure is exactly right:** IBC Chapter 17 requires a *Statement of Special Inspections* filed with the building permit, and registered Special Inspection Agencies report **directly to the building official, not to the contractor who pays them.** That is a legislated independence requirement — the strongest form of Part VI's barrier — and AASHTO accreditation gates who is accepted.

Consolidators exist at the top (NV5, Atlas, Schemmer), and the long tail is regional labs.

**Caveat:** no reliable multiples or fragmentation data surfaced. Needs a dedicated pass before it can be ranked. Flagged, not concluded.
