## 9. Kitchen hood cleaning & grease abatement (NAICS 561790) — 🟢🟢🟢 the cleanest small-cap economics found

A real listed operator, as a concrete data point rather than a market-report abstraction:

| | |
|---|---|
| Revenue | **$3.44M** |
| Adjusted EBITDA | **$725,000 — a 21% margin** |
| Recurring | **~88% of revenue** under contracts requiring periodic hood and duct cleaning |

**Twenty-one percent EBITDA margins in a "cleaning" business.** That is not a cleaning business — it is fire-code compliance that happens to involve a degreaser. NFPA 96 mandates cleaning frequency by cooking volume (monthly for high-volume/charbroiling, quarterly for moderate, semi-annual for low), enforced by local fire departments, insurers and health inspectors. Compare to commercial janitorial at 15–25% *gross* margins.

The category is explicitly described as "a defensible, regulated niche where fire-code mandates ensure recurring customer demand," with "a foundation for multi-state scale through geographic roll-ups, bundled services, and subscription-based fire compliance offerings."

**Why this is the best expression of the Part IV bundle thesis.** The same technician visit can carry hood cleaning, UL 300 suppression service, backflow testing and extinguisher inspection — all separately mandated, all sold to the same restaurant or facility manager, all currently sold by different vendors. And NFPA 96's 2025 update **requires digital documentation**, which forces the software adoption you'd be selling anyway.

**Risk:** tiny operators, owner-driven, labour is unpleasant and turnover is high. This is a 30-acquisition programme of $200k–$800k EBITDA targets, not five clean deals — which is precisely the micro-add-on financing arbitrage from Part V Finding 8.

## 10. Alarm monitoring & security integration (NAICS 561621) — 🟢🟢 and it prices the mandate directly

| Asset | Multiple |
|---|---|
| Residential alarm portfolios | **28–36x RMR** |
| Commercial monitoring | **32–42x RMR** |
| **Fire alarm & integrated security accounts** | **45–55x RMR** |
| Security integrators (EBITDA basis) | 4–8x; **7–11x+** with meaningful RMR and managed services |

**The single most useful number in this entire sweep** is that fire-alarm accounts command **45–55x RMR versus 28–36x for residential — explicitly "due to code compliance and lower attrition."** That is the mandate premium, isolated and priced by the market. Roughly a 60% uplift for the same monthly dollar, purely because a code requires it.

Stick-rate above 92% and a contract base older than 36 months justifies the top of the range. Security M&A rose 24.1% YoY in 2025 and carried into 2026; Pye-Barker is buying here too, which links this directly to the fire & life safety platforms.

**Verdict:** strong, and it validates the whole mandate thesis with a hard comparable. But RMR pricing means sellers know exactly what they have — this is an efficient market, not a discovery.

## 12. Utility line construction (NAICS 237130) — 🟢🟢 strongest demand tailwind in the sweep

Two platform formations inside seven months of 2026:
- **Front Line Power Construction** — strategic investment from Ariel Alternatives (Feb 2026), explicitly positioned for "unprecedented demand for electrical grid improvements driven by aging infrastructure, weather-related events and industry-wide talent shortages"
- **Gemspring / Key Line Construction + Palouse Power** (July 2026) — a Pacific Northwest utility infrastructure platform

The tell: the combined Key Line/Palouse business is **250+ people and is described as "one of the few regional contractors capable of self-performing both electrical and civil utility projects at scale."** At 250 employees. That is how fragmented this is, in a sector facing grid modernisation plus data-centre load growth.

**Scarce complement:** linemen. The talent shortage is named as a demand driver in the sponsors' own language, and it is driving utilities toward outsourced O&M — exactly the dynamic where value relocates to whoever holds the crews.

**Risk:** utility MSA concentration, prevailing wage/union exposure, and heavy equipment capital.

## 13. Marine construction & services (NAICS 237990/488310–390) — 🟢 clean multiple ladder

| Tier | Multiple |
|---|---|
| Sub-$2M EBITDA, single-state | **4.0–5.5x** |
| $2–5M regional with $25M+ bond line | 5.5–7.0x |
| $5–10M multi-state with **Jones Act vessels** | 7.5–9.0x |
| $10M+ with a true regional moat | **9.0–11.0x** |

Bulkhead and seawall work is the most fragmented sub-vertical (single-county contractors); pier and wharf is the highest-multiple (7.5–9.5x) because of long-cycle public-owner contracts. Saltchuk took Great Lakes Dredge & Dock private at $1.5B EV in April 2026; Blackstone owns Carrix/SSA Marine.

**Why it fits Part VII:** Jones Act vessels and bonding capacity are transferable permissions; waterfront position and licensed mariners are physically scarce. **A clean 4x → 9x ladder purely on scale and bonding** — no AI required for the arbitrage, with estimating and permitting as the AI-addressable burden.

## 14. Contract packaging / co-manufacturing (NAICS 561910) — 🟢

Sponsors describe it as having "exactly the profile sponsors want: stable, recurring volume, fragmented competition, ample whitespace for roll-ups." Platform investment up ~86% YoY, sponsor deal count up ~62% YoY.

**Multiples:** contract manufacturing 5–8x; packaging 7–10x; **a diversified SQF-Level-3 co-man with capacity headroom and QoE-ready EBITDA clears 8–10x.** Active platforms: MSI Express (Nonantum) — grew from ~450k to 2.5M+ sq ft via add-ons — Massman (Granite Equity), Smyth (Crestview), ProAmpac (Pritzker).

**Note the timing signal:** a large cohort of PE-owned packaging platforms is reaching end-of-hold, forcing both sale processes *and* a wave of add-ons. Sentiment for 2026 is "fairly tepid" with multiples expected to reflect that — i.e. entry prices softening while forced sellers appear.

**Fit:** SQF/BRC certification and installed line capacity are the scarce assets; scheduling, changeover planning and quoting are the burden.

## 15. Industrial laundry & linen supply (NAICS 812331/812332) — 🟡 downgraded

$21.9B market, but **Cintas agreed to acquire UniFirst for $5.5B EV in March 2026**, creating an entity holding nearly half the market and potentially forcing divestiture of local routes and branches.

Route density is explicitly the whole model. But with the #1 buying the #2, Vestis "sandwiched," and a fragmented tail of family laundries, this is now a consolidation endgame rather than an opening. **The one interesting angle: forced divestitures.** If antitrust requires Cintas to shed local routes, those become available to a buyer with a plant — a narrow, time-boxed opportunity worth watching.

## 16. Ambulance / EMS (NAICS 621910) — 🟡 downgraded on payer and precedent

$22.1B industry growing 2.2% CAGR. Consolidation logic is sound — spreading dispatch, compliance and collections costs over higher call volume lets consolidators underbid smaller rivals on multi-year municipal contracts, and cities are increasingly outsourcing 911 and non-emergency transport.

**But the cautionary precedent is severe:** KKR's Envision Healthcare — parent of Global Medical Response/Rural Metro — **filed Chapter 11 in 2023 under post-LBO debt.** Reimbursement is payer-set, surprise-billing legislation has reshaped the economics, and the sector attracts political attention ("how private equity captured the ambulance market" is a genre now).

**Verdict:** the operational thesis is right, the payer and political risk fails the Part VII test. Skip.

## 17. Real estate appraisal (NAICS 531320) — 🔴 **downgraded to a warning. This is the user's objection, proven.**

I ranked appraisal highly in the screen on the strength of Fannie Mae's Appraiser Independence Requirements — a legally mandated third party the customer cannot self-serve. The regulator is dismantling it in real time:

- **GSE appraisal-waiver share was 26% in February 2026** — over a quarter of transactions now require no appraisal at all
- Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac have introduced desktop appraisals, hybrid appraisals, waivers and AVMs
- The hybrid model **"directly addresses the appraiser shortage because the time-consuming physical inspection is handled by someone who doesn't need to hold a full appraiser license"** — i.e. the licence requirement itself is being unbundled
- FHFA continues widening waivers

**This is the cleanest possible illustration of why regulatory moats are rented.** The independence requirement still exists on paper. The *volume it protects* is being legislated away underneath it. A buyer who underwrote appraisal in 2021 on "GSEs require an appraisal" has watched a quarter of the market disappear.

Consolidation continues anyway (Accurate Group/Voxtur at $30M; Class Valuation/AppraisalTek; Nationwide/USRES) into a $7.5B market — but into a shrinking mandate.

**Verdict: eliminated, and retained in the file as the canonical example of the failure mode.**

## 18. Land surveying (NAICS 541370) — 🟡 thin data, structurally interesting

Valuation is driven by "licensed surveyor depth, crew capacity, equipment, backlog, recurring referral sources, WIP quality, and whether the owner is the only license or rainmaker" — a textbook scarce-complement profile where the licence and the crew are the asset.

**But note the entrant:** ZenaTech, an **AI drone and geospatial** company, signed multiple offers in July 2026 to acquire land surveying firms across the US, Canada and Australia, expected to contribute ~C$40M revenue in year one. That is an AI roll-up of surveying, already in motion — and it's not in the AI Roll-up Nexus directory, which means **the census in Part III undercounts.**

No reliable multiples data surfaced. Flagged, not concluded.

## 19. Background screening (NAICS 561611) — 🟡

FCRA obligations, state clean-slate laws, and the FDIC's revised Fair Hiring in Banking Act (effective Oct 2024) extend **mandatory** vetting — a genuine mandate. North America is 43.6% of global revenue. Market is "moderately consolidated" with leading providers expanding by acquisition (HireRight/ClearChecks, April 2025).

**Concern:** this is pure information work with no physical complement. Delete the AI work and you own an FCRA licence and a customer list. **Trade, not company** — same category as collections and credentialing. Also directly exposed to identity-verification APIs commoditising the input.

## 20. Heat treating, ASME code-stamped fabrication & NADCAP processes (NAICS 332811/332410) — 🟢🟢

The permission structure is unusually explicit:

- **ASME code stamps (U, S, R) are gating** for pressure vessels, boilers and repairs — you cannot legally fabricate or repair without them
- **ASME Section IX weld qualification** gates the welders themselves
- **NADCAP accreditation for special processes deepens the moat inside AS9100** — the operator language is literally "deepens the moat"

Maintaining active U, R and PP stamps alongside physical infrastructure is described as what lets a shop meet code "without third-party handoffs" — i.e. the stamp plus the plant is the competitive position.

**Why it survives the user's objection better than appraisal:** ASME stamps are maintained by a *private standards body* adopted into state and municipal code, not by a single legislature that can repeal them. Part VII classified private de facto standards as durable precisely because no legislature can vote them away — the ecosystem would have to migrate, which for pressure-vessel safety codes is not happening.

Combined with the Part VII job-shop thesis (plant + certification durable, quoting burden separable), **this is the strongest manufacturing expression found.**

## Two corrections to earlier parts

1. **The Part III census undercounts.** ZenaTech is running an AI roll-up of land surveying and does not appear in the AI Roll-up Nexus directory. Treat the directory as a floor on competition, not a ceiling.

2. **Appraisal is the proof of the rented-moat objection.** Independence requirements remain on the books while GSE waivers removed 26% of the volume and hybrid models unbundled the licence itself. Any vertical whose demand is created by a *single* regulator or GSE policy should be underwritten as a lease with a termination clause. ASME/NADCAP-style **private standards adopted into code** are materially more durable than agency policy — that distinction now looks like the most important refinement in the whole framework.
